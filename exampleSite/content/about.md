## The First Life

I was born Minoru Kageno, and I was not a remarkable person. I was a shut-in with a single fixation: becoming an eminence in shadow — a mastermind who pulls strings from behind the scenes and is never seen doing it.

So I trained. Every day, without fail, I trained toward the peak of what a human body could reach, and in public I performed mediocrity so well that nobody looked twice at me. I became a vigilante under a masked alias, and I learned the arts by which a man might be unmade.

Then I made the mistake every chūunibyō eventually makes. I decided I needed magic. I stood under waterfalls. I converted to new religions. I strapped myself to crosses. None of it worked, so in despair I struck my head against a tree — and when I saw headlights in the dark, I believed at last that my magic had come.

I walked into the truck. I was seventeen.

## The Second Life

I woke as Cid Kagenou, second son of a minor noble house that has produced Dark Knights for generations. Magic was real here. So I began again: training in magic, swordsmanship, and martial arts by night, while by day I studied my duties and let the family believe the brilliant one was my sister, Claire.

Both of us were thought to be a little unfortunate, in our different ways. Claire carried the marks of the Possessed and feared she would die young — a fear I removed for her years ago without ever telling her. And I, the second son, was written off entirely. Which is precisely the point.

## Shadow Garden

At ten years old I went out to rob bandits, as one does when funding is required for a lair. In one of their cages I found something rotting but alive, overloaded with a quantity of magic that should not have existed in a body. I took it to an abandoned house and worked on it for a month.

Under the flesh was an elf girl. When she woke, she was grateful, and gratitude needs a direction.

So I improvised. I told her the Three Heroes had slain the demon Diablos, that the demon had cursed their bloodline with its dying breath, that history had been rewritten, and that the possessed were shunned by a cult that meant to resurrect the monster. I named our enemy the **Cult of Diablos** — a name I took from a liquor bottle that happened to be behind her.

She asked for my name. I very nearly said *Stylish*.

I called her **Alpha**, and we founded **Shadow Garden**.

## What I Did Not Notice

Here is the part I find genuinely remarkable, in retrospect. I believed I was humoring a stubborn child with a story I had made up on the spot. I believed Alpha and her companions played along out of affection, reporting their operations so that I would feel my role-play had weight.

They did not play along. Shadow Garden is a real organization. It moves real money, it has broken real branches of the real Cult, and its decisions have changed the fate of nations. Alpha runs it, because Alpha is the only one of us who ever understood the assignment.

I remain entirely unaware that my "setting" has been true from the very beginning. Their reports are not well-developed fiction. They are intelligence, and I have been misreading it as a children's game.

## The Seven Shadows

I trained them myself, and I gave each of them their power.

- **Alpha** — my second-in-command, and the leader of the Seven. The only one who sees past both of my faces.
- **Beta** — Natsume Kafka, author of the *War Chronicles of Shadow-sama*. She writes down everything I say, and the world believes it.
- **Gamma** — the Brain. Weak in combat, unmatched in intellect. She runs Mitsugoshi Company, the lawful face that funds us.
- **Delta** — the Glass Cannon. Our main fighting force, and she only answers to strength.
- **Epsilon** — *Precise*. In the public she is Shiron, a pianist whose Moonlight Sonata I taught her.
- **Zeta** — reconnaissance, and the source of a faction that has plans for me that I have not been told about.
- **Eta** — chief scientist, who has built the camera and the machine that changes faces.

They believe they grew out of my childishness when I turned thirteen. They are wrong, and the reason they are wrong is one I would rather not examine.

## The Wisdom of the Shadows

I grew up in another world — one without magic, and considerably worse food. I let slip, casually, fragments of what I knew: pictures that move, a Moonlight Sonata, a camera, the principles of a building. Eta built devices from them. Gamma turned them into products. Epsilon made them into a reputation.

They call it the Wisdom of the Shadows, and they read profound strategy into remarks I made carelessly. I let them. It is, after all, what keeps the organization moving.

## On Immortality

I did not ask for this life. I have no intention of squandering it early.

Through magic I have extended my own lifespan — first to two hundred years, then to three hundred, when I calculated that Alpha would otherwise outlive me. I now estimate six hundred. This remains insufficient. An eminence in shadow ought to be eternal, so that the role can be performed again and again, in as many variations as one has patience for.

I have said, and meant, that eternal life would justify destroying the world. I did not expect that remark to be filed as policy.
